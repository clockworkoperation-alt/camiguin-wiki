Camiguin is an island province of the Philippines in the Bohol Sea, about 10 km off the northern coast of Mindanao. It is the second-smallest province in the country by both population and land area, after Batanes.[1]

The island was built by volcanoes, which is why it is called the Island Born of Fire. It is known for its sweet lanzones, the Sunken Cemetery of Catarman, and the forested Timpoong and Hibok-Hibok Natural Monument, an ASEAN Heritage Park.[1] Its volcanoes have their own page, Volcanoes of Camiguin, and its sights and traditions are on Attractions and culture.

Name

The name comes from kamagong, an ebony tree that grows near Lake Mainit in Surigao del Norte, the homeland of the Manobo people believed to be the island's first settlers. An early Spanish geography spelled the island Camiguing.[1]

The island's native language, Kinamigin, is the northernmost variety of the Manobo languages. It is still spoken by some people in Sagay and Guinsiliban.[1]

History

Before Spanish contact, the island was a trading stop for merchants from places such as the Rajahnate of Butuan. Spanish records say Ferdinand Magellan and Miguel López de Legazpi landed here in 1521 and 1565.[1] Two eruptions shaped the island's modern story: the birth of Mount Vulcan in 1871 and the eruption of Hibok-Hibok in 1951.

  1. 1521

    Ferdinand Magellan lands on Camiguin, according to old Spanish documents.[1]

  2. 1598

    The first Spanish settlement is founded in what is now Guinsiliban, where a watchtower guarded against pirates.[1]

  3. 1679

    Katagman, later Catarman, becomes the first major Spanish settlement.[1]

  4. 1871

    Mount Vulcan rises from a fissure, destroying old Catarman and sinking its cemetery into the sea.[1]

  5. 1942

    Japanese forces land and burn central Mambajao in reprisal for guerrilla activity.[1]

  6. 1951

    Hibok-Hibok erupts on 4 December. Out-migration later halves the island's population.[1]

  7. 1957

    Camiguin becomes a sub-province of Misamis Oriental.[1]

  8. 1966

    Republic Act No. 4669 makes Camiguin a separate province on 18 June.[1]

  9. 2004

    The Timpoong and Hibok-Hibok Natural Monument is created to protect the island's forest core.[1]

  10. 2015

    The natural monument is declared an ASEAN Heritage Park.[1]

Geography

Camiguin is a pearl-shaped, mountainous island ringed by a coastal road. The province also includes small islets such as White Island, reached from Agoho near Mambajao, and Mantigue Island, reached from Mahinog.[1]

Municipalities

The province has five municipalities and 58 barangays. Mambajao, the capital, holds about 44 percent of the population.[1]

Population

Camiguin's population grew steadily until the late 1940s. After the 1951 eruption, the island fell from about 69,000 people to about 34,000 as residents moved to mainland Mindanao.[1] The 1960 census still shows that drop.

Quick questions

Why is it called the Island Born of Fire?

The whole island is volcanic in origin. Wikipedia describes it as four stratovolcanoes with several flank domes, and Hibok-Hibok is still classed as active.[1]

What is Camiguin's capital?

Mambajao, which is also the largest municipality by area and population.[1]

What does the local economy depend on?

Fishing and farming, with copra historically the largest earner. Lanzones became the main crop, and tourism has grown in recent years.[1]
